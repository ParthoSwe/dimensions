Dimensions of space

A meditation on dimensions and the shadows objects cast. Each form exists in its own number of dimensions but is rendered for you in 3D — and each casts a shadow on the floor below.

In the default view the shadows are physical — flat 2D projections of the objects above, the kind a light source would actually cast. The Mathematical tableau mode shows something different: shadows that are exactly one dimension lower than the object itself.

The four objects

Line (1D), Plane (2D), Cube (3D), and Tesseract (4D). The line, plane and cube rotate within their own dimensional space. The tesseract rotates through four dimensions — including planes that don't exist in our 3D world. What you see is its 3D projection, the same way a cube drawn on paper is a 2D projection of a 3D form.

Surface

Wireframe reveals the underlying structure — edges only, no surfaces. Useful for seeing the tesseract's full topology.

Solid renders the objects as filled grey forms.

Colored assigns a unique color to each face. The plane has 1 face (1 color), the cube has 6, and the tesseract has 24. Colors are indexed: the plane uses color 1, the cube colors 1–6, and the tesseract colors 1–24, so you can trace the same color across objects.

Translucent

Switches the surfaces to half-transparent so you can see through to the back. For the tesseract this reveals how the 24 faces overlap in 3D — places where multiple faces stack appear darker.

Mathematical tableau

Pauses all rotation and arranges each object so its shadow is one dimension lower than the object itself.

The line stands vertically and casts a point. The plane stands on its edge and casts a line. The cube rests with edges along the axes and casts a square. The tesseract is shown in its characteristic cube-within-a-cube form — and its shadow is rendered as a simple cube, the simplest 3D projection of a 4D form.

The tableau makes the staircase visible: point → line → square → cube. Each shadow is the same shape as the object one dimension below it.

Manual rotation

Turning this on pauses the automatic rotation and lets you rotate the objects yourself. Hover over an object to highlight it, then drag to rotate. Each object only rotates within its own dimensional space — the line cannot rotate at all, the plane has one rotation plane, the cube three, and the tesseract six.

Drag horizontally to rotate around the vertical axis, drag vertically to tilt forward and back. Shift+drag (or middle-click drag) rotates around the third axis.

Rotations propagate from higher to lower dimensions: dragging the cube also rotates the tesseract in the shared planes; dragging the plane rotates the plane, cube and tesseract together.

For the tesseract's two extra rotations into the fourth dimension, use the circular 4D rotation pad below its label. Drag horizontally for xw, vertically for yw. These rotations only affect the tesseract — the other objects don't have a fourth axis to rotate into.
